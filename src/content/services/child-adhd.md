ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder) is a neurodevelopmental
condition. That means it affects how the brain develops and works, in this case
how a person regulates their attention, their impulses and how active they are.
It usually begins in childhood and can carry on into adult life. An ADHD
assessment is how a psychologist works out whether ADHD explains what you are
seeing, and what would help.

## Signs parents notice

Every child is different, and none of these on its own means a child has ADHD.
Parents usually come to us because several of them have been going on for a
while, at home and at school:

- trouble focusing or concentrating, and making careless mistakes
- great difficulty with tasks they do not enjoy, yet a high capacity for tasks they do
- being physically restless, talking a lot, or a mind that never seems to slow down
- often losing belongings
- interrupting others or blurting out answers
- struggling to follow instructions or finish tasks

A teacher may have raised it first. Sometimes the worry is about something else,
such as anxiety or falling behind at school, and attention turns out to be part
of the picture.

## How long it takes

Most assessments take three to four sessions, counting the first meeting with
you (the intake) and the feedback session at the end. Sessions are booked around
your family and the clinician, and around how long your child can focus: one
longer session with breaks, or several shorter ones. The steps are set out
below.

## Who carries out the assessment

Therapy House has fully registered psychologists and provisional psychologists
(psychologists who are completing the final stage of their training) who are
trained in assessment. Only provisional psychologists with relevant training
take on assessment work, and **every report is signed off by a fully registered
psychologist**. Reception will suggest a clinician once they know your reason
for seeking assessment.

## What to bring

Anything you already have that describes how things have been going:

- a referral, if you have one
- school reports
- NAPLAN results, if your child has sat NAPLAN (the national literacy and numeracy tests in Years 3, 5, 7 and 9)
- any earlier assessment reports
- notes from other help your child has had, such as reading intervention, occupational therapy or a speech assessment
- any letters about earlier diagnoses

## Paediatricians, medication and funding

Psychologists cannot prescribe medication. If you are considering medication
for your child, talk with your GP. A paediatrician or psychiatrist will usually
need to be involved.

If you are applying for funding or support, a diagnosis of ADHD will usually
need to be confirmed by a paediatrician or psychiatrist. If a paediatrician or
psychiatrist has already referred you, tell us when you call. We can contact
them to ask whether they need the assessment done in a particular way.

If something new comes up during the assessment, a member of the team will
talk with you about adding to the package. Nothing changes without your
consent.

## ADHD and autism

ADHD and autism can occur together, and some signs overlap. If you are also
wondering about autism, see our
[autism assessment for children](/assessments/child-autism/). The clinician will
talk with you at intake about which assessment fits your question.

If the main worry is reading, writing or maths, see our
[learning assessments](/assessments/learning/) for dyslexia, dysgraphia and
dyscalculia.

## After the assessment

The report describes your child's strengths as well as their difficulties, and
it makes recommendations for home and school. If you would like help putting
the recommendations into practice, a psychologist can work with you and your
child. Our page on
[psychology for children and teenagers](/children/) explains how that works.

Autism is a neurodevelopmental difference. That means it shapes how the brain
develops and works: how a person communicates, how they relate to other people,
and how they take in sound, touch, light and other sensations. Autism is sometimes
only noticed in adult life, particularly in people who learned to hide their
differences by copying how others behave. An adult autism assessment is how a psychologist
works out whether autism explains your experience.

## Who this is for

This page is for adults who suspect they are autistic. People seek an
assessment for different reasons:

- to understand themselves better, or to have their experience recognised
- to get practical recommendations and strategies
- to ask for adjustments at university, TAFE or work
- to apply for funding or support, such as through the NDIS (National Disability Insurance Scheme)

## Signs that lead adults to seek assessment

None of these on its own means you are autistic. Adults usually come to us
because several of them have been true for as long as they can remember:

- social communication feels different for you, or more effortful than it seems to be for others
- reading other people's facial expressions, tone of voice and body language takes conscious work
- noise, light, textures or busy places are overwhelming, or you seek out particular sensations
- you rely strongly on routine, and changes to plans cause real distress
- social situations make you anxious, or you become overwhelmed easily

## Who assesses you

Therapy House has fully registered psychologists and provisional psychologists
(psychologists who are completing the final stage of their training) who are
trained in assessment. Only provisional psychologists with relevant training
take on assessment work, and **every report is signed off by a fully registered
psychologist**. The tools used depend on you. One the clinic uses is the MIGDAS-2
(Monteiro Interview Guidelines for Diagnosing the Autism Spectrum), a
conversation-based assessment that can be used at any age.

## What to bring

Autism is present from early childhood, so the clinician needs a picture of you
as a child as well as now. Bring what you can find:

- your old school reports
- a referral, if you have one
- any earlier assessment reports
- any letters about earlier diagnoses

If a parent, older sibling or someone else who knew you as a child can complete
a questionnaire, that helps. It is fine if nobody can. Tell the clinician and
they will work with what is available.

## Funding and your psychiatrist

If you need the diagnosis for funding, such as the NDIS, it will usually need
to be confirmed by a psychiatrist. If a psychiatrist has already referred you,
tell us when you call. We can ask them whether they need the assessment done in
a particular way.

## Autism and ADHD

Autism and ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder) are different
conditions, but they can occur together and some signs overlap.

- **Autism** is mainly about social communication, sensory experience and a
  strong need for routine and predictability.
- **ADHD** is mainly about regulating attention, activity and impulses.

Both can make it hard to focus, to cope with change or to manage everyday
organisation, which is why they are sometimes confused. If attention is also a
question for you, see our [adult ADHD assessment](/assessments/adult-adhd/).
The clinician will talk with you at intake about which assessment fits your
question, or whether to look at both.

## By video or in the room

Assessment sessions are held in person at the clinic in The Gap. Ask reception
whether any part of your assessment could be done by video.

A child psychologist helps children and teenagers with the feelings and
behaviour that are getting in the way at home, at school or with friends. At
Therapy House that work happens in a house in The Gap, and parents are part of
it from the first appointment.

## Ages we see

The psychologists here see children, teenagers and their families. Our group
programs are built for ages 6 to 15, and the Circle of Security parenting
program is for parents of children from 4 months to 12 years. If your child is
very young, call reception and they will tell you who can see them.

## What children and teenagers come for

Children and teenagers come to us with [anxiety](/therapy/anxiety/) and
fears, school avoidance (finding it hard, or impossible, to go to school),
low mood, trouble with friends, low self-esteem, big emotions that are hard to
calm down, and the effects of grief, family change or frightening experiences.
Some come because a parent or teacher has noticed something and nobody is yet
sure what it is.

## Play therapy

Younger children often cannot explain what is wrong in words, so they show it
in play. Play therapy uses toys, games, drawing and pretend play as the
language of the session. Through play a child can express and work through
worries, practise calming down, and try out new ways of responding. It is
usually used with children aged about 3 to 11. Older children and teenagers
mostly talk, sometimes with drawing or activities alongside. Our library
article on [play therapy](/library/play-therapy-therapy-made-for-children-with-childrens-minds-in-mind/)
explains more.

## How parents are involved

For most children the first appointment is with the parents alone. It gives
you room to talk openly about your worries, your child's strengths and your
goals, without your child listening. Your child usually comes to the second
session, where the psychologist spends time getting to know them and helping
them feel safe in the room.

Goals are set together with you and, depending on age, with your child. The
younger the child, the more the work happens through parents. With teenagers,
what the psychologist shares with parents depends on the young person's age
and their ability to consent, and the psychologist explains how that will work
at the start. The article
[parent involvement in child therapy](/library/parent-involvement-in-child-therapy/)
answers the questions parents ask most.

## Working with your child's school

When it would help, your child's psychologist can talk to their teacher or
school, with your consent. Meetings, letters and reports are charged in
addition to sessions; reception will tell you the cost first.

## ADHD support for children

Therapy for a child with ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder)
usually focuses on the day-to-day: routines, managing frustration and big
feelings, friendships, and confidence. Parents learn strategies to use at home
as well. Psychologists do not prescribe medication; a paediatrician (a doctor
who specialises in children) does that, and an assessment report can be part
of that conversation.

## Family therapy

Sometimes the whole family is under strain, not one child. Family therapy
brings family members into the room together to understand what is happening
and change how you respond to each other. See [family therapy](/therapy/family/).

## Assessments for children

If you want to understand how your child learns or what is behind their
difficulties, an assessment gives you a clear answer and a written report:

- [ADHD assessment for children and teenagers](/assessments/child-adhd/)
- [autism assessment for children and teenagers](/assessments/child-autism/)
- [learning assessment](/assessments/learning/) for dyslexia, dysgraphia and dyscalculia
- [cognitive and giftedness assessment](/assessments/cognitive/)

Families with NDIS funding can read our [NDIS page](/ndis/).

## Groups, and Circle of Security for parents

[Group programs](/group-therapy/) teach emotion regulation and social skills to
children and young people of similar ages. For parents, the
[Circle of Security parenting program](/group-therapy/circle-of-security-parenting-program/)
helps you understand what your child needs from you and how to respond.

## Where families come from

Families come from across Brisbane, and many from the suburbs around the
clinic: The Gap, Ashgrove, Bardon, Keperra, Ferny Grove and Mitchelton. There
is plenty of street parking.

Teenagers who live further away, including in regional Queensland, can have
sessions by video from home. For a younger child, reception can talk through
whether video or the room would suit better.

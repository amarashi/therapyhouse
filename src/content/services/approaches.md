Psychologists use structured, tested ways of working, called approaches or
therapy models. You do not need to choose one before you come. Your
psychologist will suggest what suits you, your child or your relationship,
and will often draw on more than one. This page explains each approach the
clinic's psychologists are trained in, so that the names make sense when you
hear them.

<h2 id="cbt">CBT (cognitive behaviour therapy)</h2>

**What it is.** CBT looks at the links between what you think, how you feel
and what you do. You learn to notice unhelpful thoughts ("I will make a fool
of myself", "nothing ever works out"), test them against what actually
happens, and change the habits that keep a problem going, such as avoiding
things you fear. It is practical and focused on the present, and there is
usually something to practise between sessions. CBT is used with children and
adults; with younger children it is adapted with games, drawings and help
from parents.

**What it helps with.** [Anxiety](/therapy/anxiety/), including panic, social
anxiety, separation anxiety and OCD (obsessive-compulsive disorder);
[depression](/therapy/depression/); and, in its trauma-focused form,
[trauma and PTSD](/therapy/trauma/).

**Who uses it here.** CBT is one of the clinic's core approaches. Ask
reception which clinician would suit you or your child.

<h2 id="act">ACT (acceptance and commitment therapy)</h2>

**What it is.** ACT is a newer relative of CBT. Instead of arguing with
difficult thoughts and feelings, you learn to notice them, make room for them
and let them pass, without letting them decide what you do. At the same time
you get clear about what matters to you, and take small steps towards it.

**What it helps with.** [Anxiety](/therapy/anxiety/),
[low mood and depression](/therapy/depression/), stress, and living with
long-term difficulties that cannot simply be fixed.

**Who uses it here.** [Laura Storey](/team/laura-storey/) draws on ACT in
her work with adolescents and young adults.

<h2 id="emdr">EMDR (eye movement desensitisation and reprocessing)</h2>

**What it is.** EMDR is a structured therapy for memories that still feel
raw. You bring a memory to mind for short periods while also following
side-to-side eye movements, taps or sounds. Over several sessions the memory
usually becomes less vivid and less upsetting. Preparation comes first, so
that you have ways to steady yourself before any memory work begins, and you
do not have to describe the memory in detail.

**What it helps with.** [Trauma and PTSD](/therapy/trauma/) (post-traumatic
stress disorder). With trauma-focused CBT, it is one of the two therapies with
the strongest research support for PTSD in the Australian PTSD guidelines.

**Who uses it here.** [Dr Amelia Shay](/team/amelia-shay/) offers
EMDR-informed trauma work.

<h2 id="eft">EFT (Emotionally Focused Therapy)</h2>

**What it is.** EFT was developed for couples and is also used with families
and individuals. It treats most relationship conflict as a problem of
connection: under the criticism or the silence, each person is asking whether
the other is there for them. EFT helps you see the cycle you get caught in,
understand the feelings and needs underneath it, and respond to each other in
ways that build trust.

**What it helps with.** [Couples and relationship problems](/therapy/couples/),
family conflict, and the emotional distance that can grow between people who
love each other.

**Who uses it here.** Ask reception which clinician offers EFT.

<h2 id="schema">Schema therapy</h2>

**What it is.** Schema therapy combines ideas from CBT, attachment and other
therapies. It looks at deep, long-standing patterns, called schemas, that
often began in childhood when an important need was not met, such as a sense
that you are not good enough or that people will leave. It helps you
recognise when an old pattern is being triggered, meet the need behind it in
healthier ways, and break cycles that keep repeating in relationships.

**What it helps with.** Long-standing difficulties that have not shifted with
shorter therapies, including patterns in relationships, persistent low
self-worth, and chronic anxiety or depression.

**Who uses it here.** [Laura Storey](/team/laura-storey/) draws on schema
therapy in her work.

<h2 id="psychodynamic">Short-term psychodynamic therapy</h2>

**What it is.** Psychodynamic therapy explores how past experiences, and
patterns you may not be fully aware of, shape your feelings and relationships
now. The relationship with the therapist is part of the work: patterns that
show up there can be noticed and understood as they happen. Short-term
psychodynamic therapy does this within an agreed number of sessions, focused
on one central problem.

**What it helps with.** At Therapy House it is offered for deeper problems
that can show up as anxiety, depression, personality difficulties and
relationship problems. Our library article on
[long-term psychodynamic therapy](/library/long-term-psychodynamic-therapy/)
explains the longer form.

**Who uses it here.** Ask reception which clinician offers psychodynamic
therapy.

## Not sure which is right?

You do not need to know. Call reception and describe what is going on; they
will suggest a clinician, and the clinician will talk with you in the first
session about the approach they would use and why.

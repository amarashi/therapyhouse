*By Jazmin*

![Jazmin Squire](/assets/library/jazmin.webp)

Depression is more than occasional sadness. It is a serious and complex mood disorder that affects how we think, feel, and function day to day. Unlike occasional sadness, clinical depression or Major Depressive Disorder can feel relentless, draining, and deeply isolating.

The good news? **Depression is treatable**. Alongside professional therapy and medication, simple, consistent lifestyle changes can play a powerful role in managing symptoms.

Below are 12 expert-backed tips from behavioural therapists to help you support your mental health and emotional well-being.

## 1. Get moving

Exercise increases endorphins, improves energy, and can be just as effective as antidepressant medication for mild to moderate depression.

Start small:

- Take a 10-minute walk
- Stretch or do yoga at home
- Dance to music you enjoy

Aim for 30 minutes a day, but even short bursts of movement matter. Additionally, evidence suggests that doing your exercise outside in nature and/or with a friend is as effective as antidepressants for treating depression.

## 2. Eat to nourish your mood

What you eat affects how you feel. Processed, sugary, and high-fat foods can contribute to low mood and fatigue, while whole foods support mental clarity and emotional balance.Gut health matters—over 90% of serotonin (your feel-good brain chemical) is produced in the gut.

Try to include:

- Fresh fruit and vegetables (especially avocados)
- Whole grains
- Lean protein and oily fish (like salmon)
- Fermented foods or probiotics

## 3. Hydrate for mental clarity

Dehydration can mimic or worsen symptoms of depression. Ditch the sugary and caffeinated drinks and aim for 2–3 litres of water per day.

Not a fan of plain water? Try:

- Flavoured sparkling water
- Herbal teas (chamomile, ginger, hibiscus)
- Water with lemon, lime, or fresh mint

## 4. Improve sleep quality

Poor sleep both contributes to and is a symptom of depression. Prioritize a healthy sleep routine to help your brain and body recharge.

Tips for better sleep:

- Go to bed and wake up at the same time daily
- Keep your bedroom cool, quiet, and screen-free
- Avoid food and caffeine 2–3 hours before bed

Supplements like magnesium may also help promote restful sleep, but consult with your doctor before introducing supplements into your routine.

## 5. Journal to process emotions

Writing helps you clarify your thoughts, express emotions, and recognise negative patterns. Try journaling a few minutes each day.

Prompt idea: “What recurring thoughts or feelings am I experiencing today?

## 6. Challenge negative thoughts

Depression can distort thinking. You may believe things like “I’ll always feel this way” or “Nothing ever works out.”

Counter this with questions:

- Is this thought based on facts?
- Have I felt differently before?
- What’s a more helpful way to look at this?

Remember: **You are not your thoughts and thoughts are not facts**.

## 7. Interrupt the Rumination Cycle

Rumination (replaying distressing thoughts or events) is common in depression. Try to notice when it’s happening and gently redirect your attention.

Try:

- Doing something physical
- Listening to music
- Calling a friend
- Practicing mindfulness or breathwork
- Going outside and breathing in the fresh air and watching the trees in the wind or the bees on the flowers, or looking at the stars.

## 8. Refresh your routine

Stuck in a rut? Depression thrives on monotony. Even small shifts can rewire your brain’s reward system and lift your spirits.

Start small:

- Walk around the block after work
- Rearrange your space
- Cook a new recipe

## 9. Build a healthy routine

On the other hand, a lack of routine can create chaos. Establishing structure, no matter how simple, can ground you and reduce emotional overwhelm.

Ideas:

- Get dressed each morning
- Schedule one meaningful activity
- Set regular mealtimes and breaks

## 10. Set small, realistic goals

Feeling stuck? Break large tasks into small, manageable steps.

**Example:** Instead of “clean the house,” try “fold laundry” or “wipe kitchen counter.”

Celebrate small wins – they build momentum and confidence!

## 11. Stay connected

Isolation fuels depression. Even if it’s hard, reach out to people you trust. You don’t need to face everything alone.

Try:

- Texting a friend
- Calling a family member
- Joining a support group or online community

## 12. Practice joy, gratitude and self-compassion

Depression often makes it hard to enjoy life’s small pleasures. Actively create space for what lifts your spirit.

Simple joys to try:

- Watch a comedy
- Play with a pet
- Sip a favourite tea
- Volunteer or help someone else
- Write down 3 things you’re grateful for

Laughter and gratitude both increase dopamine, a key neurotransmitter for mood regulation.

## Final thoughts

Managing depression doesn’t require huge changes overnight. But small, consistent steps, taken daily, can shift the way you feel and function.

If you’re struggling, know this: you are not alone. Support is available, and healing is possible. Talk to a therapist or mental health professional, and start integrating some of the practices above into your day. Every step counts.

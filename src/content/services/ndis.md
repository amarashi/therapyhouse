The NDIS (National Disability Insurance Scheme) funds supports for people
with a permanent and significant disability. If your plan or your child's
plan includes funding for therapy, you can use it to see a psychologist at
Therapy House. Sessions can be in person in The Gap or by video.

## Who can use NDIS funding here

How you can use your funding depends on how your plan is managed. The NDIS
offers three ways:

- **Self-managed.** You pay the clinic's invoice and claim the money back
  from the NDIS yourself. Therapy House sees self-managed participants.
- **Plan-managed.** A plan manager, an organisation you choose, pays the
  invoice for you from your plan. Therapy House sees plan-managed
  participants.
- **Agency-managed** (also called NDIA-managed). The NDIA (National Disability
  Insurance Agency, which runs the NDIS) pays providers directly, and only
  providers registered with the NDIS can be used. If your plan is
  agency-managed, call reception before booking to check whether the clinic
  can see you under it.

If you are not sure how your plan is managed, it says so in your plan, or
your support coordinator or plan manager can tell you.

## What is provided

Psychology sessions funded by the NDIS are usually claimed from the
Capacity Building budget in your plan, under the support category for
improved daily living. At Therapy House that covers:

- **Ongoing therapy appointments**, for children, teenagers and adults. For
  what therapy with a child involves, see
  [psychology for children and teenagers](/children/).
- **Help with assessments needed for NDIS applications.** An assessment can
  give a diagnosis and a clear picture of a person's needs, which an NDIS
  application or plan review may ask for. See [assessments](/assessments/)
  for the process and what each one involves.

Eligible participants may also be able to use their funding for the
[Circle of Security parenting program](/group-therapy/circle-of-security-parenting-program/);
check with your plan manager or support coordinator.

## Reports for plan reviews

Before a plan is reviewed, the NDIS often asks for a report from each
therapist describing the supports provided, the progress made and what is
recommended next. Your psychologist can write this report. Like other reports
and letters, it takes the clinician's time and is charged at the clinic's
hourly rate, so it is worth budgeting for in your plan.

## How to start

1. Call reception, or [get in touch](/contact/), and say that you or your
   child are an NDIS participant.
2. Tell reception how your plan is managed, and have your NDIS number and
   your plan manager's details (if you have one) ready.
3. Reception will suggest a clinician and talk you through availability.
4. The clinic sends you a service agreement, which
   sets out the supports, the fees and how invoices are paid. It is sent to
   you privately rather than published here, because it is specific to you.

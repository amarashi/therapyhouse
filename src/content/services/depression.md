Depression is more than a bad week. It is a low mood, or a loss of interest
in things, that stays for weeks and starts to affect sleep, work, study and
relationships. It is common, it is not a weakness, and it can be treated.
Talking with a psychologist is one of the main treatments.

## What depression and low mood can look like

Depression shows up differently in different people. Common signs include:

- feeling sad, empty or flat for most of the day, nearly every day, for two
  weeks or more;
- losing interest or pleasure in things you used to enjoy;
- sleeping much more or much less than usual;
- changes in appetite or weight;
- tiredness and low energy, even after rest;
- trouble concentrating or making decisions;
- feeling worthless, guilty or a burden to others;
- thoughts that life is not worth living.

"Low mood" is a gentler term for some of the same experiences. You do not
need to meet a checklist to get help. If you have been feeling this way for a
while, it is worth talking to someone.

## How therapy helps

Therapy gives you a regular, private place to make sense of what is happening
and to change the patterns that keep a low mood going. Several approaches are
used here:

- **CBT (cognitive behaviour therapy)** looks at the links between thoughts,
  feelings and actions. It helps you catch the harsh, hopeless thoughts that
  come with depression and test them, and slowly rebuild the activities that
  give life shape and enjoyment.
- **ACT (acceptance and commitment therapy)** helps you make room for painful
  thoughts and feelings without letting them decide what you do, and to take
  steps towards what matters to you.
- **Short-term psychodynamic therapy** explores deeper patterns, often from
  earlier in life or in relationships, that can show up as depression.

Your psychologist will talk with you about which fits. More about each is on
[the therapies we use](/therapy/approaches/).

## Teenagers and adults

In teenagers, depression often looks like irritability, anger or withdrawal
more than sadness: spending more time alone, dropping friends or activities,
falling behind at school, or sleeping at odd hours. Parents are often the
first to notice. With teenagers, the psychologist works with the young person
and agrees with them and their parents how parents are kept involved.

In adults, depression often builds slowly, after a loss, a change, a long
stretch of stress or for no reason you can name. Help with depression is one
of the things [adults come to us for](/adults/).

## When to see a GP first

It is a good idea to see your GP (general practitioner, your family doctor)
as well as a psychologist. A GP can:

- check for physical causes of low mood and tiredness, such as thyroid
  problems, anaemia or side effects of medicines;
- talk with you about whether medication could help, as psychologists do not
  prescribe;
- write a Mental Health Treatment Plan and referral, so that your sessions
  attract a Medicare rebate. The current rebate amounts are on the
  [fees page](/fees/).

If you are having thoughts of ending your life, do not wait for an
appointment: call Lifeline on 13 11 14, or 000 in an emergency, or go to your
nearest hospital emergency department.

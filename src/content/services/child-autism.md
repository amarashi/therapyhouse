Autism is a neurodevelopmental difference. That means it shapes how the brain
develops and works: how a person communicates, how they relate to other people,
and how they take in sound, touch, light and other sensations. An autism
assessment is how a psychologist works out whether autism explains what you are
seeing, and what support would help your child at home and at school.

## Signs parents notice

Every child is different, and none of these on its own means a child is
autistic. Parents usually come to us because several of them have been going on
for a while:

- differences in social communication, such as finding conversation or play with other children hard
- differences in reading and using nonverbal cues, such as facial expressions, tone of voice and gestures
- difficulty taking in sensory information, such as noise, clothing textures or busy places
- relying strongly on routine, and becoming very distressed when it changes

A teacher, a child care educator or a GP may have raised it first. Sometimes a
child is already seeing a psychologist for anxiety or behaviour, and autism
becomes part of the question.

## How the assessment works

The package includes an intake session with you (your child may come too,
depending on their age), time with your child, questionnaires for you, your
child's teacher and, for older children, your child, then scoring, the written
report and a feedback session. The tools used depend on your child. One the
clinic uses is the MIGDAS-2 (Monteiro Interview Guidelines for Diagnosing the
Autism Spectrum), a conversation-based assessment that can be used at any age.

Most assessments take three to four sessions, counting the intake and the
feedback session. Sessions are booked around your family, the clinician and how
long your child can focus for. The steps are set out below.

## Who carries out the assessment

Therapy House has fully registered psychologists and provisional psychologists
(psychologists who are completing the final stage of their training) who are
trained in assessment. Only provisional psychologists with relevant training
take on assessment work, and **every report is signed off by a fully registered
psychologist**.

## What to bring

- a referral, if you have one
- school or kindy reports
- NAPLAN results, if your child has sat NAPLAN (the national literacy and numeracy tests in Years 3, 5, 7 and 9)
- any earlier assessment reports, including speech or occupational therapy assessments
- notes from other help your child has had
- any letters about earlier diagnoses

## Paediatricians and funding

If you are applying for funding, such as through the NDIS, an autism diagnosis
will usually need to be confirmed by a paediatrician or psychiatrist. If a
paediatrician or psychiatrist has already referred you, tell us when you call.
Some paediatricians ask for a particular test, such as the ADOS-2 (Autism
Diagnostic Observation Schedule). We can contact your paediatrician to ask what
they need, so the assessment gives you what you are looking for. Read more on
our [NDIS page](/ndis/).

If something new comes up during the assessment, a member of the team will
talk with you about adding to the package. Nothing changes without your
consent.

## Autism and ADHD

Autism and ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder) can occur together,
and some signs overlap, such as difficulty with attention or with changes to
plans. If attention is also a concern, see our
[ADHD assessment for children](/assessments/child-adhd/). The clinician will
talk with you at intake about which assessment fits your question.

## After the assessment

The report describes your child's strengths as well as the areas where they
need support, with recommendations for home and school. If you would like help
putting them into practice, see
[psychology for children and teenagers](/children/).

If you think you or your child might have ADHD, this article explains how to get diagnosed with ADHD in Australia: who can diagnose it, what the assessment involves, what to bring, and how costs and Medicare work. ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder) is a condition of brain development that begins in early childhood. It can also be diagnosed for the first time in teenagers and adults.

## Who can diagnose ADHD?

In children, ADHD is usually diagnosed by a paediatrician (a doctor who specialises in children's health), a child psychiatrist (a medical doctor who specialises in mental health) or a psychologist. Adults are usually diagnosed by a psychiatrist or a psychologist.

The Australian ADHD clinical practice guideline, written by the Australasian ADHD Professionals Association (AADPA), says the assessment interview is usually done by clinicians experienced in developmental and mental health conditions, such as paediatricians, psychiatrists and psychologists.

General practitioners (GPs) are taking on a bigger role. In Queensland, GPs have supported children with ADHD since 2017, and since 1 December 2025 specialist GPs in Queensland can start, change and continue ADHD stimulant medicine for adults. The rules on who can prescribe ADHD medicine are set by each state and territory, so they differ depending on where you live.

A psychologist can assess and diagnose ADHD, but cannot prescribe medicine. If medicine is an option you want to consider, a doctor (a GP, paediatrician or psychiatrist, depending on your state and your age) will be part of the process.

## The steps to a diagnosis

**1. See your GP.** For most people the first step is a visit to the GP. Your GP can talk through your concerns, check for other causes of the symptoms, and refer you to a psychologist, paediatrician or psychiatrist. You need a referral from a doctor to see a paediatrician or psychiatrist.

**2. Gather your history.** ADHD is diagnosed only when the signs started in childhood. For a diagnosis, symptoms must have:

- started before the age of 12
- lasted for more than 6 months
- caused real problems in at least two areas of life, such as home and school, or home and work
- got in the way of everyday life.

For adults, this means showing that the signs were there when you were young. You may be asked for old school reports.

**3. The assessment.** An ADHD assessment is mostly a detailed interview, plus questionnaires. According to the AADPA guideline, it usually includes:

- a clinical interview about your symptoms, when they started, how much they affect you, and your strengths. A full interview can take 2 to 3 hours and may be spread over more than one session.
- your developmental, family, health, school and work history
- rating scales (standard questionnaires) filled in by you and by people who know you well, such as a parent, partner or teacher
- checks for other conditions that can look like ADHD or often come with it, such as anxiety, depression, learning difficulties or autism.

Children are usually seen separately from their parents for part of the assessment. Brain scans and computer tests are not needed to diagnose ADHD. Extra testing, such as a cognitive or learning assessment, is added only when there is a reason to check for a learning or thinking difficulty.

**4. The feedback session.** The clinician explains whether you or your child meet the criteria for ADHD, and what the next steps are. That might be therapy, support at school or work, or a referral to a doctor to talk about medicine.

## What to bring

It helps to have these ready before the assessment starts:

- a referral letter, if you have one
- school reports (old ones, for adults)
- any earlier assessments or reports from other health professionals
- for children, reports or notes from teachers or the school
- a list of current medicines and health conditions.

## How much does an ADHD assessment cost?

Costs vary a lot between clinicians and between public and private services, so ask for a written quote before you book.

- **Public services.** Paediatric clinics in public hospitals are covered by Medicare, so there is nothing to pay.
- **Private paediatricians and psychiatrists.** You need a referral. Medicare covers some of the cost, and specialists generally charge a gap fee (the amount you pay on top of what Medicare pays back). Fees depend on what the specialist charges, so ask for an estimate before the first visit.
- **Private psychologists.** Psychologists set their own fees, and almost all charge a gap fee. A mental health treatment plan from your GP gives a Medicare rebate for up to 10 individual sessions a year of psychological treatment. Ask the clinic before you book whether any part of an assessment attracts a rebate.

## After a diagnosis

Treatment is planned around the person. It can include psychological therapy such as cognitive behaviour therapy (CBT, a practical therapy that looks at how thoughts, feelings and actions are connected), family counselling, social skills training, support at school, and medicine. Stimulant medicines are usually the first type of medicine prescribed for ADHD, and a doctor may combine medicine with therapy.

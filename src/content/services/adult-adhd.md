ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder) is a neurodevelopmental
condition. That means it affects how the brain develops and works, in this case
how a person regulates their attention, their impulses and how active they are.
It begins in childhood, but many people are not assessed until they are adults.
An adult ADHD assessment is how a psychologist works out whether ADHD explains
the difficulties you have been living with.

## Who this is for

This page is for adults, including university and TAFE students, who wonder
whether they have ADHD. People seek an assessment for different reasons:

- to understand themselves better, or to have their experience recognised
- to get practical recommendations and strategies
- to ask for adjustments at university, TAFE or work, such as extra time in exams or flexible hours
- as a step towards discussing treatment, including medication, with a doctor

## Signs that lead adults to seek assessment

None of these on its own means you have ADHD. Adults usually come to us because
several of them have been true for a long time, often since school:

- trouble focusing on tasks you do not enjoy, while finding it easy to focus on ones you do
- organising your diary, paperwork or bills feels impossible, even when you manage complex work
- losing things often
- interrupting people, or acting before thinking
- a restless body or a mind that will not slow down
- starting everything very early to meet deadlines, then feeling exhausted or behind anyway

Many adults with undiagnosed ADHD have built ways to cope, such as working much
longer hours than their colleagues. That can work for a while, but it can also
lead to perfectionism, burnout, anxiety or low mood. Anxiety and mood problems
often occur alongside ADHD.

## Who assesses you

Therapy House has fully registered psychologists and provisional psychologists
(psychologists who are completing the final stage of their training) who are
trained in assessment. Only provisional psychologists with relevant training
take on assessment work, and **every report is signed off by a fully registered
psychologist**. Reception will suggest a clinician once they know why you are
seeking assessment.

## What to bring

ADHD starts in childhood, so the clinician needs a picture of you as a child as
well as now. Bring what you can find:

- your old school reports (adult ADHD assessments usually need these)
- NAPLAN results, if you have them
- a referral, if you have one
- any earlier assessment reports
- any letters about earlier diagnoses

If a parent, older sibling or someone else who knew you as a child can complete
a questionnaire, that helps. It is fine if nobody can. Tell the clinician and
they will work with what is available.

## Medication and your GP or psychiatrist

Psychologists cannot prescribe medication or advise on it. If you are
considering medication, take your report to your GP. A psychiatrist will
usually need to be involved as well. If you need the diagnosis for
funding, a psychiatrist will usually need to confirm it.

If a psychiatrist has already referred you, tell us when you call. We can ask
them whether they need the assessment done in a particular way.

## By video or in the room

Assessment sessions are held in person at the clinic in The Gap. Ask reception
whether any part of your assessment could be done by video.
